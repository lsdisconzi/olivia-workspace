The Core Criminal Evidence: The Fabrication Instruction

Two segments from the phone call at the beginning of the transcript constitute the core criminal evidence for document falsification:

### SEG 36 (86.29s-92.54s) — "Can you adjust it?"

> [[SPK-diego-latam-supervisor]]: "Y en esa carta de contingencia podra ajustar como para el te digo yo?"

**Translation:** "And in this contingency letter, can you adjust it the way I tell you?"

**The word "ajustar" (adjust/modify/tailor) is the linguistic marker of intent (dolo).** [[SPK-diego-latam-supervisor]] does not ask to "fill out with facts" or "complete with the information." He asks whether the template can be modified according to his instructions. This is direct evidence of intent to control the narrative, not to record truth.

Legal mapping:

  • CPCL Art. 197 (Falsificacion en instrumento privado) — The core act of falsification: asking to "adjust" a document to suit a predetermined narrative.
  • CPCL Art. 193.4 (Falta a la verdad en narracion de hechos sustanciales) — [[SPK-diego-latam-supervisor]] asserts editorial control over what the document will say.

    • ### SEG 39 (97.75s-110.35s) — The Operational Instruction

    **Translation:** "Something to show? Yes, if the format says it's being written, write what Anto says and then they'll send it to sales, she tells you what mail and then they print it here. Okay? Okay friend, now yes!"
