Summary

This 23-second bridge transcript is deceptively simple but legally significant. After initially refusing to document anything (STG-26: "no es necesario"), [[SPK-diego-latam-supervisor]] pivots and offers "a document of what you wanted." The passenger accepts, and [[SPK-diego-latam-supervisor]] agrees ("Ya."). [[SPK-diego-latam-supervisor]]'s subsequent reversal in STG-28 and STG-29 is therefore not incapacity — it is a deliberate choice, and an aggravated violation based on induced reliance.

Legal Significance

1. **Induced reliance**: [[SPK-diego-latam-supervisor]]'s agreement creates reliance. His subsequent refusal (STG-28: "tu sabras el por que," STG-29: "ni siquiera corresponde que yo le entregue una carta") is an aggravated violation — the passenger waited hours based on [[SPK-diego-latam-supervisor]]'s promise.

2. **Capacity confirmed**: [[SPK-diego-latam-supervisor]]'s "Ya" confirms he has the authority and capacity to provide documentation. His later claim that it "doesn't correspond" is therefore knowingly false.

3. **Framing "what you wanted"**: [[SPK-diego-latam-supervisor]] frames the document as giving the passenger what he "wants" (demands), not as a truthful recording of events. This framing persists through STG-29, where the document is treated as a "solution" rather than a legal obligation.

**Provisions engaged:** LPDC Art. 23 bis (continuing violation). [[SPK-diego-latam-supervisor]]'s agreement and subsequent reversal constitute bad faith and aggravated violation under the induced-reliance doctrine.
