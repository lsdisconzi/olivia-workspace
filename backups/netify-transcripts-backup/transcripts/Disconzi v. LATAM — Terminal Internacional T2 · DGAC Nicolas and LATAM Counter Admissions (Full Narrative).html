Legal Significance

1. **"PUEDE QUE SEA FALSA PERO"** — The single most damaging LATAM admission outside the smoking-gun STG-7. LATAM acknowledges the accusation might be false — and enforces the lifetime ban anyway. This converts the ban from negligent reliance on false information to knowing enforcement of potentially false allegations.

2. **DGAC Nicolas confirms judicial path**: A Chilean DGAC official independently confirms the passenger has sufficient evidence for a valid legal claim. This is a government official's assessment of case merit.

3. **DGAC confirms LATAM must rebook**: The official states LATAM "te tienen que reprogramar" at no cost to the passenger. LATAM's failure to do so is an independent violation.

4. **"QUE ALGO POR ESCRITO NO EXISTE"**: LATAM admits it has no written documentation policy at the airport. Any passenger removed from a flight and classified "disruptivo" receives zero written explanation — by design. This is a systemic due process failure.

5. **"La empresa no tiene ninguna solución para casos cuando el pasajero queda como disruptivo"**: LATAM admits it has NO SOLUTION for passengers classified as disruptive. The only path is litigation. This is a structural denial of consumer rights.

6. **"disruptivo" classification**: Made without formal process, hearing, or appeal mechanism. Based on an accusation LATAM itself acknowledges "PUEDE QUE SEA FALSA."

7. **DGAC Nicolas — new named individual**: A DGAC official whose testimony about what DGAC advised the passenger and what LATAM's obligations were would be material evidence.
