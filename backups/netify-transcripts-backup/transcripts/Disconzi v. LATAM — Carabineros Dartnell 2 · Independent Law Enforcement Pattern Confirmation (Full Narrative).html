Summary

This is arguably the single most important Carabineros recording and one of the highest-probative-value pieces of evidence in the entire case. A male Carabinero officer at the Dartnell sub-station — independently, without prompting — confirms that LATAM's false-aggression-report practice is **systemic and routine**. His key admissions: (1) passengers are detained for aggression reports "casi todos los días" (almost every day), (2) "ellos siempre hacen eso" (they always do this), (3) he has previously told other detainees to file formal complaints against LATAM, and (4) he recommends the passenger file a formal complaint ("hágale una denuncia a ellos"). This transforms the case from an individual incident into evidence of **institutional practice**, directly supporting class-action potential and establishing a pattern of conduct relevant to both criminal (CHIPENCOD) and consumer (LPDC) claims. Meanwhile, [[SPK-passenger-leandro]] recounts the full chronology: false accusation by [[SPK-stewardess-accuser]] → PDI CCTV exoneration → narrative shift from "aggression" to "entered the gate alone" → falsified unsigned document after hours of insistence.

Legal Significance

This recording is **TIER 1 evidence** for the following reasons:

1. **Independent law enforcement confirmation of systemic pattern**: A Carabinero officer — not connected to the passenger, not connected to LATAM, not connected to PDI or DGAC — independently volunteers that LATAM's false-aggression-report practice is routine and daily. This is the closest the case has to an independent expert witness on institutional practice.

2. **Transforms individual claim into institutional pattern**: The officer's statements — "casi todos los días," "ellos siempre hacen eso," his prior advice to other detainees — establish that the passenger's experience is not isolated but representative of LATAM's standard operating procedure. This supports:

  • **Class action potential**: If this happens "almost every day," there is a class of affected passengers
  • **Criminal liability**: Pattern evidence supports CHIPENCOD claims by showing systematic falsification, not isolated error
  • **Consumer claims**: LPDC claims are strengthened when the conduct is shown to be institutional rather than individual

    • 3. **Narrative shift documented**: The passenger's account captures the exact moment LATAM shifted its justification from "aggression" (disproven by CCTV) to "entered the gate alone" — a completely different allegation. This shift is independently corroborated by STG-20 ("Nadie te acuso por agresion") and latam_STG_2 (the carta de desembarque alleging "ingresa la aeronave sin autorizacion").

    4. **Audio recording confirmed**: The passenger states "Yo tengo grabación de audio de eso todo" — establishing the existence and provenance of the audio recordings that form the case's evidentiary backbone.

    5. **Formal complaint recommended by law enforcement**: The male officer's "hágale una denuncia a ellos" is a direct recommendation from a law enforcement officer to pursue legal action against LATAM — powerful evidence that trained police officers recognize the conduct as unlawful.

    6. **The officer's prior advice to other detainees**: "le dije a una persona que pasó detenida la otra vez acá, le dije haga una denuncia" — this establishes a chain of prior victims, each told by the same officer to file complaints. These prior detainees are potential witnesses or co-plaintiffs.
